# PAA V0.9 — User Acceptance Test Protocol

## Purpose
Test whether first-time users can understand and complete PAA without coaching, whether the BOI discriminates intelligibly among operators, and where the product produces confusion, disagreement, abandonment, or technical failure.

## Minimum pilot
Start with 12–20 participants who did not help design PAA. Do not teach them the operator meanings before the BOI. Give each participant an anonymous code only.

## Core session
1. Complete onboarding and consent.
2. Enter the birth data requested by the app.
3. Complete all 36 BOI scenarios without outside explanation.
4. Read the convergence/divergence result.
5. Submit feedback even when the result feels accurate.
6. Record at least one lived Anamnesis episode.
7. Optional: commit one prospective prediction and resolve it later.

## Research discipline
- Do not coach participants toward an operator.
- Do not explain ambiguous BOI choices during completion; ask the participant to flag the item instead.
- Preserve divergence and negative feedback.
- Never reinterpret a frozen prediction after the outcome.
- Separate usability success from theoretical validation.
- Do not market BOI scores as psychological diagnosis, clinical assessment, or established predictive validity.

## Acceptance gates for the next release
A release should not advance toward commercial packaging until:
- the app installs and launches reproducibly on target iOS and Android test devices;
- the core flow can be completed without researcher intervention;
- no critical data-loss or navigation defects remain;
- confusing BOI items are identified and revised through recorded feedback;
- completion/abandonment is measurable;
- exports contain no intentional direct identifiers;
- at least one second test round confirms that fixes improved the observed problems.

## What success means
UAT can establish usability and reveal methodological weaknesses. It cannot by itself establish that birth-derived glyphs predict human behavior. That claim requires a separately designed validation study with preregistered hypotheses, adequate sample size, controls, and out-of-sample testing.
