# PAA Commercialization & Licensing Gates

This document separates **product readiness** from **evidence readiness**. PAA can become polished, useful, and licensable before every scientific question is settled, but commercial claims must never outrun the evidence.

## Gate 1 — UAT-ready product
- Core Discover → Observe → Test flow completes without defects.
- BOI choices remain blinded.
- Interruption/resume works.
- In-context feedback works.
- Anonymous UAT export works.
- Accessibility and device-layout defects are triaged.
- Tester consent and prototype data handling are explicit.

## Gate 2 — Repeatable research instrument
- Operator canon/version is frozen per study cohort.
- Scenario item bank is versioned.
- Exposure counts and scoring rules are documented.
- Test–retest stability is measured.
- Confusion patterns among operators are quantified.
- Inter-rater rules exist for coding lived episodes.
- Prospective predictions are timestamped/frozen before outcomes.

## Gate 3 — Evidence-backed claims
Claims must be tiered:
1. **Descriptive:** what the PAA framework defines or what the app records.
2. **Observed:** what a study sample actually did.
3. **Predictive:** what PAA forecast before outcomes and with what accuracy.
4. **Validated:** only after appropriate independent/repeated studies support the claim.

Do not collapse resonance, convergence, or anecdotal accuracy into validation.

## Gate 4 — Institutional product
Before licensing to schools, employers, health providers, counselors, governments, or other high-impact institutions:
- define intended and prohibited uses;
- conduct legal/privacy review for each target market;
- establish role-based access and audit logging;
- add secure backend, encryption, retention, deletion, and export controls;
- document model/instrument limitations;
- create administrator and participant manuals;
- separate research mode from production interpretation mode;
- add contract-ready terms, support, versioning, and change-control policies.

## Gate 5 — IP and licensing package
A serious licensing package should contain:
- trademark/brand inventory;
- copyrighted operator canon, item bank, manuals, and visual system;
- documented scoring methodology and version history;
- license tiers and permitted-use language;
- research dossier and known limitations;
- implementation guide/API specification if offered to institutions;
- pricing model, support obligations, and renewal/version policy.

## Governing principle
**The commercial moat should be the method, evidence discipline, instrument design, longitudinal dataset, and implementation system—not an unsupported claim of mystical certainty.**
